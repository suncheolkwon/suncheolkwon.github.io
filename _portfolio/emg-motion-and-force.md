Surface electromyography (sEMG) records electrical activity from muscles through electrodes on the skin. In one study, we recorded from three hand muscles and used an artificial neural network to estimate palmar pinch force in real time. In another, we combined sEMG with joint angular velocities to estimate upper-limb motion as a person moved.

A later study examined how prolonged muscle contractions affected the recognition of wrist movements in people who had experienced a stroke. Participants performed wrist movements at 20% of their maximum voluntary contraction. Classification accuracy remained consistent under those test conditions.
