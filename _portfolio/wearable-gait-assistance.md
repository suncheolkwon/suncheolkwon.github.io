A wearable robot for walking must accommodate movement as well as produce joint torque. In our hip-exoskeleton study, the frame was symmetrical, but only the right hip was powered. An angle-based controller timed assistance to hip flexion while the wearer could move the unpowered side freely. We used this setup to study how the muscles of both legs responded to one-sided assistance.

At the ankle, our work addressed a different problem. People with foot drop can have difficulty lifting the front of the foot during a step. Ten participants walked both barefoot and with a pneumatic ankle-foot orthosis that lifted the foot during the swing phase. Motion capture showed less asymmetry in lower-limb joint motion during assisted walking.
